1. "addons" folder contains "lowpoly_terrain_editor": plugin.cfg, plugin.gd and its sub-folders (core, styles, editing, mesh, materials, generation, runtime, biomes, ui). Copy the whole folder.

2. Drag and drop the "addons" folder into your Godot project directory / root. 
if you already have the "addons" folder, you can drag and drop / copy only the "lowpoly_terrain_editor" folder and paste it into your "addons" folder.

3. In Godot's project, Click "Project" -> "Project Settings" -> "Plugins" -> Enable the plugin

4. Create a new 3D Scene (e.g. Node3D). Click it, and click "New Terrain" in the 3D editor menu bar.
   The new terrain uses the Senegal Savanna biome (Inspector > Look > Biome to change it) and is empty: the 3D view says so until you drag in it to add ground (Ground is already active), or click "Generate Map" in the Inspector. Ground you paint stays bare: add grass, trees and props afterwards with the Vegetation tool (one thing at a time, and nothing to compute while you shape the ground). Generate Map grows them on the whole map it makes. The ground is walkable at once (Inspector > Game > Walkable Collision, on for new and generated terrains; tick it on a terrain made before this version if your player falls through).
   The tools are 7 icons in the 3D editor menu bar, shown once a terrain is selected (hover one for its name): Ground (with its list of ground styles), Relief, Flatten, Road, River, Waterhole, Vegetation (with its list of plants). Undo / Redo: Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Shift+Z. With a tool active, a ring under the mouse shows where it acts and its size (inner ring = half strength); it turns orange when Ctrl+drag with Ground is over a road / river.
   Ground: drag to add ground in the style picked in the list next to it (the biome's ground styles; "Custom" = your own colours in Inspector > Advanced > Custom Ground Style). Ctrl+drag removes ground, and also roads, rivers and waterholes under the brush. Relief: drag to raise, Ctrl+drag to dig. Rivers never flow uphill: a river drawn over a hill cuts a channel through it at the height of its lower side, instead of climbing it (it still runs down a slope); raising or digging the ground near a river updates its channel.
   Keys in the 3D view, with a tool active:
     Ctrl+drag  inverse tool (Ground: remove, Relief: dig, Vegetation: fewer)    Shift+drag  smooth
     [ ]  brush size (with Road / River: width of the next road / river)    - =  strength
     Esc  leave the tool (back to normal selection)
   Alt+drag and right / middle mouse still move the camera while a tool is active.
   Brush size and strength are remembered for the project (next editor session too).
   The terrain Inspector has four short sections in the order of a first map: Map (preset, seed, Generate Map), Look (biome, details, Smooth Ground, your own materials), Roads, Rivers & Waterholes (what the tools draw next) and Game (collision, Runtime Build, draw distance, mobile water, ground shadows, detail seed). Everything else is in the closed "Advanced" section: Terrain Size, Shading, Heights & Offsets, Custom Ground Style, Road Look, River Water, Waterhole Shape, Details (size, "Details Everywhere") and Pixel Map (Import from Image). Hover a section title or a setting for its explanation.
   The biome is the terrain's look: ground styles, baked shading (style blend, slope tint, ambient occlusion, color variation, cliff darkening), waterhole colors and what grows. "Look > Edit Biome" (or "Advanced > Shading > Edit Ground Shading") opens it; every change shows on the terrain at once. A biome file (e.g. senegal_savanna.tres) is shared by every terrain that uses it: "Copy Biome to This Terrain" gives one terrain its own copy, stored in the scene, to change it alone (Ctrl+Z undoes it). Without a biome, "Create Editable Biome" puts an editable Temperate copy in the terrain. Scenes made with an older version are converted when opened: a terrain whose shading or waterhole colors had been tuned by hand gets its own copy of the biome holding them, so it looks the same (save the scene to keep the conversion).
   Ground look: Inspector > Look > "Smooth Ground" (on by default) blends the ground triangles together (no triangle pattern). Untick it for the faceted low-poly look. Same performance either way; cliffs stay faceted.
   Ground texture: the plugin only paints colors; the texture is your game's choice. Put your own material in Inspector > Look > "Ground Material" (it must use the vertex color as albedo). Seen from a player's eyes, a plain colored ground looks like a smooth plastic sheet: test_project/world/looks/ground_detail.tres is an example that adds a fine soil grain (fading out in the distance) and large soft patches on top of the biome colors.
   Textured ground styles: a ground style (biome > Ground Styles, or Advanced > Custom Ground Style) can have a Top Texture and a Side Texture with their Tile Size (metres per repeat). Up to 7 textured ground styles are blended with each other and with the colored styles like the colors are (the biome's Blend and Blend Noise: no stair-step border along the cells; where two meet, each pixel shows the one with the most relief there, so pebbles and cracks poke through instead of two photos fading into each other) and keep the baked shading (ambient occlusion, color variation, cliff darkening). In a waterhole basin the texture keeps its grain but takes the basin color (dried clay). From about 25 m to 90 m away the textures fade into their average color, so their repeat does not show from far or from above. Their faces are drawn by one built-in shader (one extra draw call per chunk, not one per texture); a custom Ground Material does not apply to them. Import the textures with mipmaps ("Mipmaps > Generate", VRAM Compressed), or they shimmer in the distance. Details skip textured ground by default: untick "Skip Textured" in a detail layer to grow it there. Far terrain blocks use the style's Top Color: pick one close to the texture's average color. Example and comparison: test_project/tools/texture_test.gd, test_project/results/textures/ (Poly Haven textures, CC0).
   "Ground Bumps" (Inspector > Look, 0.3 m on new terrains, 0 = flat; 0 on terrains made before this version) adds small bumps of about that height all over the ground, so the ground is not a dead flat sheet. They are written in the heights: collision, grass, trees, roads and rivers follow, and the Relief tools work on top (Flatten and Smooth flatten them where you use them). Changing the value only changes the bumps, never your relief; Ctrl+Z undoes it. Generate Map and the terrain growing while you paint keep the same bumps.
   Your game's own look: Inspector > Look > "Ground Material" and "Detail Material" replace the built-in materials (toon, unshaded, palette, retro...), applied at once without rebuilding. The colours and baked shading stay in the vertex colours: use "Vertex Color > Use as Albedo" + "Is sRGB" on a StandardMaterial3D, or read COLOR in a shader (convert it from sRGB, see the example). Ground Material = untextured ground, cliffs and waterholes (textured styles keep their texture); Detail Material = every detail layer except your own Models and custom meshes with "Use Mesh Materials". A custom Detail Material has no built-in wind unless its shader moves the vertices. Roads and rivers keep their own materials. Examples (game side, not in the plugin): test_project/world/looks/ (ground_toon, ground_unshaded, detail_toon, detail_unshaded, detail_toon_wind with its shader, and outline.tscn: cartoon outlines as a post-effect, put it under your Camera3D). Comparison of 9 looks: test_project/results/styles/ (tools/style_test.gd).

5. African terrains: a new terrain already uses biomes/presets/senegal_savanna.tres (dry grass, laterite, acacias, baobabs, termite mounds, skulls); for a green temperate look, pick biomes/presets/temperate.tres in the terrain Inspector, "Look" > Biome. The "Waterhole" tool places a dried waterhole with cracked mud on click (Ctrl+drag with Ground inside it removes it); its settings are under "Roads, Rivers & Waterholes" (depth, mud plate size) and "Advanced" > "Waterhole Shape"; its clay and crack colors come from the biome (Waterholes group), the same for every waterhole of the terrain.
   The biome's detail layers say what grows, how dense, on which ground and slope. On a generated map they grow by themselves; on ground painted by hand nothing grows until you use the Vegetation tool. Scenes made before this version keep "Details Everywhere" on (Inspector > Advanced > Details): there, everything grows on all ground, also on ground painted later; untick it to switch such a terrain to the new way (its decor then stays only where you paint). To add or change what grows, use the "Vegetation" tool (tree icon, last in the toolbar): drag for more (rare layers like trees up to x25 = real forest, grass up to x4; painted thicker, a layer also grows on other ground, on a bit steeper slopes and at any height; never on roads, rivers, waterhole water or cliffs; per layer: Biome > layer > Advanced > "Paint Relaxes Rules"), Ctrl+drag for fewer then none (clearings, fields, a village square), Shift+drag to go back to normal (the biome density on a generated map, bare on ground painted by hand). Undo works. The list next to the tool picks what you paint: "All plants" (grass, shrubs, trees; rocks, gravel, termite mounds and bones stay as they are) or one layer of the biome only (e.g. Acacias, Rocks, Termite Mounds: pick them to add rocks, mounds or bones on bare ground). With a tree or prop layer picked, one click grows one where you click (a baobab, a termite mound...; at a cliff edge, on the flat ground next to it); drag to plant more. They are spaced so they do not overlap: a click right next to one of the same kind (closer than about its size) adds none, and a forest painted thick keeps its trees about one crown apart (crowns barely touch; per layer: Biome > layer > Advanced > "Paint Spacing", in widths of the model, 0 = they may overlap). Which layers "All plants" moves: Biome > layer > Advanced > "Vegetation Brush" (Auto / Follow / Ignore). Paint for one layer is kept by its name: renaming the layer drops it.
   The built-in props (acacia, baobab, termite mound, zebu skull, dead tree, shrub, rocks, grass) are modelled in Blender: tools/blender/lte_props.py rebuilds them from code (paste it in Blender's Scripting tab, then build_all() and export_glb(...)), and tools/blender/glb_to_gdscript.py turns the GLB into addons/lowpoly_terrain_editor/mesh/detail_mesh_data.gd. A scene saved with "Save everything" keeps the props it was saved with until its details are rebuilt (Generate Map, or any change to the biome); with "All at runtime" they are rebuilt when the scene opens.
   Lighting: the plugin does not set up the sky, fog or sun (each game lights its scenes its own way). For the savanna look, drop test_project/world/savanna_sky.tscn (WorldEnvironment + warm sun 24 degrees high, so the ground facets stand out) in your scene, or use test_project/world/savanna_environment.tres on your own WorldEnvironment (light haze in the distance, natural colors). On phones, sun shadows cost the most: give your sun 2 shadow splits (Directional Shadow > Mode) and a 50 m Max Distance, like savanna_sky.tscn (a third fewer triangles drawn than 4 splits / 80 m, still sharp close by). Roads and rivers cast no shadow (they lie flat on the ground). For low-end phones, untick Inspector > Game > "Ground Casts Shadows": hills no longer cast shadows on the plain (trees, rocks and props keep theirs), about 20% fewer triangles drawn and roughly 10% less GPU time on the savanna map.

6. Whole maps from a seed: in the terrain Inspector, "Map" > pick a "Map Preset" in the list (Senegal Savanna, Temperate Island; with none picked, Generate Map uses the preset of the terrain's biome, else the first one), set a Map Seed and click "Generate Map" (Undo restores the previous terrain). "Custom Recipe..." at the end of the list takes your own recipe file. If the map is not on screen, the camera moves to show all of it (same view angle). Same recipe + seed = same map; duplicate a recipe to tune it (plateaus, lowlands, ground patches, tracks, waterholes). You can keep editing the map by hand afterwards.
   Your own decor (trees, huts, rocks, props made in Blender or bought): import the file in Godot (.glb, .gltf, .blend, .fbx, or a .tscn scene), then Look > Edit Biome > Detail Layers > add a layer (or open one) > Mesh > drag the file into "Model". It is drawn with its own materials and textures, at its real size in metres (Shape > Scale to vary it), standing on the bottom centre of the model ("Keep Model Origin" to use the model's own origin instead). The whole model becomes one mesh: still one draw call per material and chunk, however many copies. Give it a name: it appears in the Vegetation list, so you can place it by clicking (rare layers) or paint it, and set Collision > Collision Radius for trees or buildings the player must not walk through. Big flat-bottomed models on slopes: lower Placement Rules > Slope Max, or use Advanced > Sink. Example: test_project/assets/test_models/hut.glb (tools/make_test_model.gd, checked by tools/check_custom_model.gd). A plain Mesh resource still goes in "Mesh" (size in terrain cells).
   In game: the terrain gets a "_LowpolyTerrainRuntime" child. It gives trees / baobabs / termite mounds a collision (detail layers with "Collision Radius" > 0). Tick "Generate Map" on it to build a new map from its recipe + seed when the game starts (procedural levels; call build(seed) from code for another one). Spawn your player on its "terrain_ready" signal.
   Map edge: the plugin builds the terrain only; what lies past the border is up to your game. FPS / third person: a far landscape (example: test_project/world/map_horizon.gd, a MapHorizon child of the terrain with distant ground, hills, trees and invisible border walls). Top-down: keep the camera inside the map and put a large plain ground (same colour) under it. Island: water all around.
   Wind: grass, shrubs and trees (built-in meshes) sway in the wind, in the editor and in game; rocks, mounds and bones stay still. From code: MaterialFactory.set_wind(Vector2(direction_x, direction_z), strength) (strength 0 = calm, 1 = default, 2 = strong). Scenes built before keep working: their details get the wind when the scene loads.
   Tip: save big maps as .scn (binary) instead of .tscn: about 5x smaller and 10x faster to open.
   The terrain data (heights, ground styles) is stored compressed in the scene (the "_LowpolyTerrainRuntime" child unpacks it when the scene loads: keep that node). With "Save everything", most of the size of a generated map is the saved meshes / details / collision (about 19 MB of text for a 193 savanna map, 160 KB with "All at runtime").
   "Runtime Build" (Inspector > Game) chooses what the scene file stores: "All at runtime" (default for new terrains: only the terrain data, a few hundred KB; meshes, details, roads and collision are rebuilt when the scene opens or the game starts, spread over frames), "Details at runtime", or "Save everything" (biggest scene, fastest loading; terrains made before this version keep this mode).
